Visit Kamru Fort in Sangla, then proceed to Chitkul—the last inhabited village near the Indo-Tibet border. Walk along the turquoise Baspa River and enjoy traditional wooden Kinnauri architecture.

Navigate the Ka loops to Nako village and lake (3,660 m). Safety & Logistics

Practical Spiti Advisory: Ground Realities Before You Fly

Vital logistical facts covering passes, altitude sickness, cash, and communication.

Kunzum Pass & Chandratal Seasonal Closure

Full Circuit Season: The complete loop including Chandratal Lake operates strictly between late June and mid-October [MONTHS].

Winter / Spring Note: Kunzum Pass (14,931 ft) receives up to 20 feet of snow in winter and is cleared by the Border Roads Organisation (BRO) around early June. If you travel between November and May, you can still visit Kaza via the Kinnaur route, but you must return back via Kinnaur to Shimla, as Chandratal and the Manali exit remain completely inaccessible.

Altitude Sickness Advisory (AMS)

Gradual Ascent: The primary reason our loop starts from Shimla rather than Manali is safe acclimatization. Traveling via Kinnaur raises your elevation gradually over 5 days before you sleep in Kaza (3,800m).

Precautions: Stay hydrated (drink 3 to 4 liters of water daily). Avoid strenuous exercise and alcohol during the first 48 hours. Garlic soup is widely recommended in local homestays. Consult your family physician regarding Diamox before leaving Delhi or Bengaluru. All our circuit cabs are equipped with an emergency oxygen cylinder.

Mobile Network & Internet Coverage

Postpaid BSNL & Jio 4G: Work reliably in Rampur, Reckong Peo, Kalpa, and Kaza town.

Airtel & Vi: Work up to Sangla and Reckong Peo, but have extremely erratic or zero signal in Kaza and remote villages.

Complete Blackout Zones: There is zero mobile network between Losar, Kunzum Pass, Chandratal Lake, Batal, and the North Portal of Atal Tunnel. Inform your family beforehand that you will be offline during Days 8 and 9.

ATM Cash Advisory & Inner Line Permits

Carry Sufficient Cash: The last dependable ATMs are located in Reckong Peo and Kaza. However, Kaza ATMs frequently run out of cash during peak tourist season. Homestays and local cafes do not accept UPI or credit cards due to patchy internet. Carry at least INR [CASH AMOUNT] in physical banknotes per person.

Permits: Indian nationals do not require an Inner Line Permit. Foreign nationals must obtain an Inner Line Permit (ILP) for the Khab-Sumdo stretch [OWNER TO VERIFY CURRENT RULES]. Carry 4 passport copies and 4 passport-size photographs.

Frequently Asked Questions About the Spiti–Kinnaur Circuit

Key details for travellers planning the full 10-day circuit.

How many days does the Spiti–Kinnaur circuit take?

A safe, memorable Spiti–Kinnaur circuit requires 9 to 11 days starting from Shimla and concluding in Manali or Chandigarh.

Attempting this 1,200 km trans-Himalayan expedition in fewer days is dangerous due to rapid elevation gains that can trigger severe Acute Mountain Sickness (AMS). By spending nights in Sarahan, Sangla, Kalpa, Tabo, Kaza, and Chandratal, daily driving is restricted to 4 to 6 hours, allowing you to acclimatize comfortably, explore ancient monasteries without rushing, and enjoy photography in daylight. If you have extra buffer days, you can add a tranquil stay in Chitkul or an extra night in Kaza for the Pin Valley national park. Read our day-by-day plan above.

Which vehicle is best for Spiti Valley roads?

The Toyota Innova Crysta is the gold standard for the Spiti–Kinnaur circuit thanks to its proven 178 mm ground clearance, strong ladder-frame chassis, and spacious interior.

While hatchbacks and compact sedans can reach Kinnaur, they frequently damage their underbodies on the unpaved boulder roads and water crossings between Kaza, Kunzum Pass, Batal, and Gramphu. For families of 4 to 6 passengers, the Innova Crysta offers unmatched comfort on rugged corrugated terrain. For larger group tours or adventure trekking parties, our 12-seater Force Tempo Traveller provides the required suspension durability and luggage capacity. Compare all options on our Fleet Comparison page.

When is the Spiti road via Kunzum Pass open?

Kunzum Pass is officially motorable only from late June to mid-October [MONTHS], subject to snow clearance by the Border Roads Organisation.

For the rest of the year, Kunzum Pass (14,931 ft) remains closed under heavy snow, preventing circular access between Kaza and Manali. During the summer and early autumn window, travellers can complete the entire loop and camp near Chandratal Lake. If you plan to visit Spiti between November and May, you can still access Kaza through the Shimla-Kinnaur road, but you must return along the same Kinnaur route. We verify live road status directly with the Lahaul-Spiti district administration before every departure.

Do I need a permit for Kinnaur and Spiti?

Indian citizens do not need an Inner Line Permit for Kinnaur or Spiti, while foreign passport holders require an official Inner Line Permit (ILP) [OWNER TO VERIFY CURRENT RULES].

Indian citizens only need to show a valid government photo identity card (Aadhaar Card, Passport, or Voter ID) at police checkpoints such as Sumdo and Darcha. Foreign nationals traveling through the sensitive border zone between Reckong Peo and Sumdo must possess an approved ILP issued by the District Magistrate in Shimla or Reckong Peo. Our drivers verify all tourist documentation at the checkposts and facilitate smooth clearance. Check with our office for current documentation guidelines prior to booking.
