Shimla to Kaza Route Profile & Road Conditions

Detailed physical reality of the Hindustan-Tibet Highway so you know exactly what terrain you will experience.

Key Waypoints & Stops

The journey from Shimla to Kaza covers [KM] km and passes through diverse Himalayan landscapes:

  • Shimla to Narkanda (65 km): Dense cedar and deodar forests, smooth asphalt, apple orchards.
  • Narkanda to Rampur Bushahr (65 km): Gradual descent to the Sutlej river gorge (altitude drops to 1,000 m).
  • Rampur to Reckong Peo / Kalpa (100 km): Dramatic blasted rock cliffs, Wangtoo hydro tunnels, and entering the tribal Kinnaur district.
  • Kalpa to Nako & Chango (110 km): Spiti river confluence at Khab, switchbacks climbing to high-altitude Nako village and lake (3,660 m).
  • Chango to Tabo & Kaza (90 km): Sumdo police checkpoint, ancient 996 AD Tabo Monastery, Dhankar cliff monastery, arriving in Kaza (3,800 m).

Road Surfaces & Seasonal Advisory

Road Quality: Good paved highway from Shimla through Rampur and Powari. Beyond Powari, road widens but features unpaved gravel stretches near Akpa and Spello. Malling Nallah is a continuous water crossing subject to loose scree.

Operating Season: The Shimla-Kinnaur-Kaza route is accessible almost throughout the year, unlike the Manali-Kunzum Pass route which closes in winter. During December to March, heavy snow between Narkanda and Nako requires specialized snow chains and experienced mountain handling.

Acclimatization: Do not attempt Shimla to Kaza in one single continuous drive. A mandatory overnight stay at Kalpa (2,960 m) or Sangla allows your body to adjust safely to thinning oxygen levels.

Which vehicle is best for Spiti Valley roads?

The Toyota Innova Crysta is widely recognized as the best and most dependable vehicle for Spiti Valley roads thanks to its high ground clearance and ladder-frame strength.

While regular sedans manage the paved roads until Reckong Peo, the unpaved riverbed stretches, gravel zones, and water crossings around Malling Nallah, Chango, and the higher Spiti villages demand a vehicle with substantial ground clearance and robust suspension. When is the Spiti road via Kunzum Pass open?

The Kunzum Pass road connecting Kaza to Manali is open strictly between late June and mid-October [MONTHS], weather permitting.

If you book a taxi from Shimla to Kaza during April, May, or November, you can reach Kaza comfortably through the Kinnaur highway, but you must return back via the same Kinnaur route because Kunzum Pass (14,931 ft) remains closed under heavy snow. Travellers wishing to complete the full circular circuit via Chandratal Lake and descend into Manali should schedule their journey between July and September. Our team tracks Border Roads Organisation (BRO) snow-clearing advisories daily. For circuit itineraries, review our Spiti–Kinnaur circuit package.

Do I need a permit for Kinnaur and Spiti?

Indian citizens do not require an Inner Line Permit for Kinnaur or Spiti Valley, but foreign nationals must obtain a verified Inner Line Permit (ILP) [OWNER TO VERIFY CURRENT RULES].

Indian travellers need only carry valid government photo identification (Aadhaar, Passport, or Voter ID) for routine verification at the Sumdo checkpost separating Kinnaur from Spiti. Foreign nationals traveling past Reckong Peo and through Dubling towards Tabo require an Inner Line Permit, which can be processed at the District Magistrate offices in Shimla or Reckong Peo with a minimum group size of two individuals. Our drivers assist guests with document verification at every police checkpoint along the highway. Contact us to verify current district administration permit procedures before departure.

How many days does the Shimla to Kaza trip take?

A one-way taxi journey from Shimla to Kaza requires a minimum of 2 full driving days with a mandatory overnight stop in Kalpa, Sangla, or Tabo.

Attempting the [KM] km drive in a single 24-hour stretch is hazardous due to fatigue on narrow mountain bends and the sudden jump in altitude from 2,200 meters to 3,800 meters. Day one involves a 7 to 8 hour drive from Shimla to Kalpa or Sarahan (approx. 220 km), followed by day two covering the remaining 200 km to Kaza through the barren cold desert landscape. If you have extra days, adding a halt at Chitkul or Dhankar allows for relaxed sightseeing and better oxygen adaptation. Plan your dates with our online enquiry form.
