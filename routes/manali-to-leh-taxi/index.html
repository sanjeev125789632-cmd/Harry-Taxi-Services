The Manali to Leh Highway Profile & Mountain Passes

A breakdown of the 2-day expedition through some of the highest paved mountain passes on planet Earth.

Day 1: Manali to Jispa (approx. 135 km)

Route: Manali (2,050m) → Solang Valley → Atal Tunnel (9.02 km tunnel exit at 3,060m) → Sissu waterfall → Tandi (Chandra-Bhaga confluence, last reliable petrol pump for 365 km) → Keylong → Jispa (3,200m).

Highlights: Rapid entry into Lahaul Valley without the old 4-hour Rohtang Pass traffic. Gentle ascent to 3,200m in Jispa allows critical overnight acclimatization beside the Bhaga River.

Day 2: Jispa to Leh (approx. 335 km)

Route: Jispa → Darcha police checkpoint → Zingzingbar → Suraj Tal lake → Baralacha La (16,040 ft) → Sarchu border checkpost → Gata Loops (21 hairpin curves) → Nakee La (15,547 ft) → Lachung La (16,616 ft) → Pang gorge → More Plains (40 km high-altitude flat plain at 15,000 ft) → Tanglang La pass (17,480 ft) → Upshi → Karu → Leh (3,500m).

Highlights: World-renowned alpine landscapes, dramatic geological formations, and descent into the Indus Valley.

Which vehicle is best for the Manali to Leh highway?

The Toyota Innova Crysta is the premier recommended vehicle for the Manali to Leh highway due to its high ground clearance, exceptional engine reliability, and superior passenger cabin comfort.

Between Darcha and Sarchu, melting glacial snow often causes water crossings (nallahs) to swell during afternoon hours, depositing loose gravel and boulders across the road surface. Low-clearance hatchbacks and sedans risk scraping their oil sumps or stalling in freezing water. The Innova Crysta's 178 mm clearance and high torque enable our drivers to navigate these obstacles smoothly. For trekking teams or groups with extensive camping gear, our 12-seater Force Tempo Traveller provides reinforced leaf suspension and waterproof overhead roof racks. Inspect vehicle specifications on our Fleet Page.

Why is an overnight stay at Jispa necessary on the Manali to Leh route?

An overnight stay at Jispa (3,200m) is medically essential to prevent severe Acute Mountain Sickness (AMS) before attempting the 17,000+ ft passes on day two.

Attempting to drive from Manali (2,050m) straight through to Leh in a single 18-to-20 hour marathon exposes travellers to extreme altitude shock as the vehicle rapidly ascends over Baralacha La (16,040 ft) and Tanglang La (17,480 ft). Without spending a night at an intermediate elevation like Jispa or Keylong, passengers frequently experience severe headaches, nausea, dizziness, and pulmonary complications. Our drivers strictly adhere to a 2-day schedule for passenger health and safety. All our vehicles carry emergency oxygen cylinders.

What are the Ladakh taxi union rules for outside commercial vehicles?

Outside commercial taxis (including Himachal registered cabs) are legally permitted to drop passengers in Leh town, but cannot be used for internal sightseeing within Ladakh.

According to reciprocal agreements between the Himachal and Leh Taxi Unions, our taxi will pick you up at your hotel in Manali and drop you directly at your accommodation in Leh. However, for excursions inside Ladakh—such as day trips to Pangong Tso, Nubra Valley, Khardung La, or Tso Moriri—you must hire a local cab registered with the All Ladakh Tour Operators Association (ALTOA) or Leh Taxi Union.
